Six races ago this was not a fight. After the Spanish Grand Prix in September, McLaren had 306 points and Red Bull 230, a gap of 76. Since then Red Bull have scored 185 points to McLaren's 92, drew level in Austin and moved ahead in Mexico. São Paulo did not change the order: both teams scored 33, so Red Bull still lead by 17 with three races left.

## How 76 points disappeared

The turn came in Azerbaijan. Red Bull put both cars on the podium, Max Verstappen second and Isack Hadjar third, for 33 points. McLaren left Baku with nothing: Oscar Piastri was 13th and Lando Norris did not finish. It was McLaren's only weekend of the year without a point.

After that the numbers ran one way. Red Bull scored 35 in Bahrain, 34 in Singapore, 23 in Austin and 27 in Mexico. McLaren scored 10, 23, 16 and 10. The teams were level on 355 after Austin. In Mexico Verstappen won and Red Bull went 17 clear.

## Verstappen carries the most, Hadjar makes the difference

Verstappen has 279 of Red Bull's 415 points, about two thirds. He has been on the podium at every one of the last six races, with wins in Bahrain, Singapore and Mexico, and he is second in the drivers' championship. Over those six weekends he scored 134 points and Hadjar 51.

Hadjar's points matter because McLaren's second car has not kept up. He has 122 points in 17 races for Red Bull. He missed the Dutch, Italian and Spanish rounds with a wrist injury he picked up in training over the summer break, and Liam Lawson scored 14 points in his place ([Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/red-bull-confirm-hadjar-to-miss-dutch-grand-prix-as-lawson-steps-up-and-tsunoda-returns.3D3k7oc9dUwwEBZC7upjR1.3D3k7oc9dUwwEBZC7upjR1)). In São Paulo Hadjar started fifth and finished third, the fourth podium of his career and his third for Red Bull, after Monaco and Baku.

Red Bull marked it with two podium photos: Hadjar grinning with both thumbs up and a trophy in front of him, then Verstappen holding up his trophy and raising his other hand, both in Pirelli podium caps.

## McLaren's split

At McLaren the points are shared very differently. Norris has 246 and three wins: Hungary, the Netherlands and now São Paulo, from pole. Piastri has 152 and no wins this year. In the last six races he finished 13th, 6th, 5th, 7th, 13th and 6th. When Norris wins, McLaren can match Red Bull. When he does not, the team falls behind, because Red Bull now usually have both cars in the points.

McLaren were the only team to bring upgrades to São Paulo, and the package included a first look at ideas for the 2027 car ([Crash.net](https://www.crash.net/f1/news/1109304/1/mclarens-brazil-gp-upgrade-will-feature-first-look-2027-f1-car-development)). Andrea Stella said more parts would follow in Las Vegas ([GPFans](https://www.gpfans.com/en/f1-news/1092156/mclaren-next-upgrades-2026-andrea-stella-brazil-las-vegas/)).

## What each team needs

Las Vegas, Qatar and Abu Dhabi are all normal weekends with no sprint, so a team can score at most 43 points per race. McLaren need to outscore Red Bull by 18 points over three races, six a weekend on average. A Norris win with Verstappen third would be worth 10 of those on its own.

Las Vegas has been kind to Red Bull. Verstappen won there in 2023 and 2025, and George Russell won for Mercedes in 2024. Norris took pole last year but did not win.

Ferrari are 95 points ahead of Red Bull in second, which Red Bull could only close if Ferrari barely scored again, and Mercedes have already won the title. Third place is the real open fight in the constructors' table, and at 17 points it is as close as the fight for second among the drivers.
