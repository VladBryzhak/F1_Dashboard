Over the four races before São Paulo, Ferrari scored 126 points, more than any other team, and had a car on the podium every time. At Interlagos they scored six. Charles Leclerc finished eighth, Lewis Hamilton ninth and a lap down. It was Ferrari's lowest score of the season; the previous low was eight points at Monza.

```stats
title: Ferrari in São Paulo
- Points: 6
- Leclerc: grid 14, finish 8
- Hamilton: grid 20, finish 9, one lap down
- Previous four races: 126 points
source: f1db, São Paulo Grand Prix and rounds 16 to 19
```

## Out of position before the start

The weekend went wrong before the lights. Leclerc qualified fourth but had a ten-place drop for a new internal combustion engine, so he started 14th. Hamilton had a problem with his fresh engine in final practice, which forced a full power unit change; he qualified 11th and started from 20th, as our [qualifying report](/articles/sao-paulo-qualifying-chaos) explains. It was the first race this year where neither Ferrari started in the top ten.

Starting that far back is not always fatal at Interlagos. Max Verstappen started 21st and finished second ([our story on it](/articles/verstappen-interlagos-back-of-grid)). Ferrari did not have that pace.

## Tyres that would not work

Both drivers said the same thing: in wet and changing conditions, they could not get the tyres into their working window. Leclerc called his first stint "horrendous" and said the car felt like "driving on ice" ([Formula 1](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/a-complete-disaster-leclerc-and-hamilton-lament-horrendous-ferrari-performance-in-sao-paulo.sdZmqEEwBaoAC8OmBxvYh)). He ran intermediates until lap 16, then softs until lap 61, and switched back to intermediates for the last ten laps when the rain returned. That late call was right. He passed Liam Lawson for seventh on the penultimate lap, but a five-second penalty for contact with Lawson dropped him to eighth.

Hamilton's race started well. He gained 11 places on the first lap, from 20th to ninth. Then, as the track dried, his rear tyres faded and he fell back. He was one of the first to ask for slicks and ran two stints on softs, but a late spin left him ninth, a lap behind Lando Norris. He said he took some of the blame for that himself.

```stats
title: Ferrari's tyres in São Paulo
- Leclerc: intermediate 1 to 16, soft 17 to 61, intermediate 62 to 71
- Hamilton: intermediate 1 to 14, soft 15 to 38, soft 39 to 62, intermediate 63 to 70
source: OpenF1 stints
```

The weekend came straight after Mexico, where a first-lap fight between the two drivers cost Ferrari the win ([our analysis](/articles/ferrari-hamilton-leclerc-mexico)). Fred Vasseur met both of them before Brazil, and Leclerc said afterwards that "everything is settled" ([ESPN](https://www.espn.com/f1/story/_/id/50407666/charles-leclerc-ferrari-lewis-hamilton-fred-vasseur)). In São Paulo there was no fight between them. They were both too far back.

## What it cost

Before São Paulo, Hamilton was third in the drivers' championship, two points behind Verstappen in second. He left 18 behind and fourth, one point behind George Russell. Leclerc is fifth, 30 points behind Verstappen. With three races left and 75 points available to each driver, neither is out of the fight for second, but both now need help.

```stats
title: The fight for second, before and after São Paulo
- Verstappen: 261 to 279
- Russell: 250 to 262
- Hamilton: 259 to 261
- Leclerc: 245 to 249
source: f1db standings after rounds 19 and 20
```

In the constructors' table, six points helped Mercedes seal the title in Brazil. Ferrari still hold second with 510 points, 95 ahead of Red Bull. With 129 points left per team that is not yet mathematically safe, but Red Bull would need Ferrari to have two more weekends like this one.

## Next: a cold night in Las Vegas

The next race is [Las Vegas](/calendar/2026/21), a night race in November where low track temperatures make warming the tyres hard for everyone. That is exactly where Ferrari struggled in Brazil. Leclerc took pole there in 2023. If the team can fix tyre warm-up, Vegas is a chance to show that São Paulo was a bad weekend rather than a trend.
